## A. Sigils change what the atoms are

**The idea:** the circle turns parts of the molecule into elements it was dealt without.

**How it fills:** one notch for each different reflex and each different kind of runoff on a page. Full at 12.

**When full:** it is a fourth slot in the line, at any place. Using it empties it.

The six:
- **Aqua Regia** (point-down water triangle under a three-point crown). Tap a carbon with no O or N on it: one H becomes Cl. Yield x1.08 (x1.14), late rounds x1.17 (x1.32). Strip fired in 28% of rounds where it never fired before. Works.
- **Vitriol** (tilted square, upright line, seven ticks on the rim). A chain-end CH3 becomes COOH. Yield x1.05 (x1.05). Seal fired in 9% of rounds, pop in 6%. 38% of full pages had nothing to tap. Weak.
- **Sal Ammoniac** (thick eight-armed star). A chain-end CH3 becomes NH2, and its carbon leaves as carbon dioxide. Yield x0.98 (x1.01). Knit fired in 6% of rounds. 33% of full pages had nothing to tap. Fails today.
- **Sulphur** (triangle on a cross). Up to two OH become SH. Two SH 2 or 3 apart close an S-S ring and hydrogen leaves. Yield x0.97 (x1.06). Pair fired in 15% of rounds. Fails today.
- **Mercurius** (crescent, circle, cross). Every C-OH becomes C=O and every C=O becomes C-OH, all at once. Yield x1.03 (x1.21). Mixed.
- **Chrysopoeia** (a dot in a circle, four short rays). A CH2 in the middle of a chain leaves as one gold, and its two neighbours bond. Yield x1.09 (x1.17), 0.59 gold a round. Works.

**A run:** you hunt one element. You draft chlorination and Williamson, the acid homunculus is fed by fumes, and new specs on Cl, N or S open.

**Made from and feeds:**
- It wakes the five reflexes that need N, S, Cl or COOH (strip, knit, pop, seal, pair) and the reagents that make those groups.
- It feeds the slow jars: ammonia, sulfur, fumes.
- Chrysopoeia's gold buys instruments.
- Catalog entries that hold N, S or Cl come within reach.

**Biggest risk:** the dealt molecules are carbon and oxygen, so four of the six do little until molecules change.

## B. Sigils change the shape (recommended)

**The idea:** the circle bends the carbon frame: it moves, joins, cuts, feeds or copies.

**How it fills:** 12 notches, one for each reflex step. Notches carry across rounds and over-fill is kept. It fills about once a round from the middle rounds on.

**When full:** tap it before lining up a page. Using it empties it.

The six:
- **Congelatio** (two heavy arrows, one down the left, one up the right). Pick one OH or C=O and a free carbon: the group moves there. Yield x1.22 (x1.38). A new reflex fired in 37% of rounds. Works.
- **Ouroboros** (a serpent biting its tail). The two ends of the longest chain bond: one large ring. Yield x1.02 (x1.16). A new ring kind in 46% of rounds. Weak on yield, strong on shape.
- **Coniunctio** (a sun disc and a crescent joined by a thick bar). Pick two carbons that each have an H: they bond, closing a ring of any size, and one hydrogen leaves. Yield x1.05 (x1.17). A new ring kind in 46% of rounds. Works.
- **Putrefactio** (a heavy bar across the circle, the lower half solid black). Pick a C-C bond: it breaks, the smaller piece burns off as one carbon dioxide, and each cut end that stays becomes C=O. Yield x1.53 (x1.71). Strong.
- **Cibatio** (an open bowl with three heavy drops falling in). Pick a C-C bond: a CH2 goes into it, so a gap grows by one. Yield x1.10 (x1.19), tested with the CH2 also allowed to come out. Works.
- **Projectio** (circle, triangle, square, circle). Pick an OH or C=O: a copy stands on the plain carbon 4 away from it, each way. Two copies: yield x1.76 (x2.01). Pages of 10 or more steps went from 0.20 to 0.59 a round. Strongest.

**A run:** you play for gaps. Every page you ask whether one cut, one move or one join turns a dead stretch into an overreaction.

**Made from and feeds:**
- Reflex steps fill it, and its edit decides which reflexes fire next.
- It changes which lines of reagents pay.
- It opens shape specs: rings, large rings.
- Putrefactio feeds the carbon dioxide jar and Coniunctio feeds the hydrogen jar.
- Catalog: large-ring compounds such as muscone come within reach.

**Biggest risk:** Coniunctio sits close to the slime homunculus, which counts two carbons as 3 apart. They stay apart because the sigil truly bonds the two carbons.

## C. Sigils are one great work across the run

**The idea:** the circle bends the page's laws, and each time it fills the law lasts longer.

**How it fills:** one notch for each water, hydrogen, carbon dioxide or fume that leaves. 10 to a fill.

**When full:**
- First fill (black ring): its law holds for the next page.
- Second fill (white ring): for the rest of the round.
- Third fill (red ring): for the rest of the run, and the circle is closed.

The six:
- **Pelican** (the pelican flask, its two arms bending back into its body). Each water that left this page goes back as an OH on a C=C you pick. Yield x1.23 (x1.32). Works.
- **Solutio** (point-down water triangle). Every C=C next to a C=O takes water, with the OH on the far carbon. Yield x1.38 (x1.37). Strong.
- **Conversio** (a circle in quarters with an arrow round the rim). Turn forward: every OH becomes C=O, every C=O becomes C=C with water off, every C=C takes an OH. Or turn backward. Yield x1.23 (x1.37). Works.
- **Palingenesis** (a thick spiral ending in an arrow pointing out). The molecule goes back to how it stood before the page. That page's yield stays and its uses come back. Yield x1.23 (x1.15). The line was different on every page it was used. Strong but slow.
- **Caduceus** (a staff and two serpents, crossing three times). The first two reagents fire at once on the molecule as it began the page, and the first wins ties. Not run.
- **Tria Prima** (marks for sulphur, mercury and salt). This page's water counts as sulfur (sulphur), ammonia (mercury, the volatile one) or acid fumes (salt). Yield x1.82 (x1.91), but the test also fired the ammonia homunculus, so this is too high.

**A run:** you plan the whole run around one law that grows from one page to permanent.

**Made from and feeds:**
- Runoff fills it, and runoff is the jars' food too.
- It changes the order of reagents (Caduceus) and the reflexes (Solutio adds one).
- The spec fights Palingenesis, because the spec reads the final molecule.

**Biggest risk:**
- The lasting stages are not measured at all.
- Pelican sits close to the water homunculus.
- Filling from runoff shrinks the molecule.

## Order for B (held back)

It shows the sigil you hold and the next one as an outline with its count, never the whole list. Sigils are never offered in a bachelor's run. One sigil is chosen at the start of each run.

2. Ouroboros: end a page with a C=O or OH on both ends of the chain.
3. Coniunctio: close 5 rings on one page.
4. Putrefactio: 25 carbon dioxide across all runs.
5. Cibatio: fire grow on all three pages of one round.
6. Projectio: an overreaction of 12 steps on one page.

## Names, and what was fixed against the rules

**Names changed to fit their real meaning:**
- Azoth to Sal Ammoniac. Azoth is the universal solvent, not nitrogen.
- Rubedo to Coniunctio. Rubedo is a stage of the work, and sun joined to moon is the picture of the conjunction.
- Tetrasomia to Conversio. Tetrasomia means the four base metals; turning element into element is "conversion".
- Nigredo, the cutting version, to Putrefactio, and the water version to Solutio. Two sigils shared one name.
- The Stone to Projectio, the casting of the Stone.
- Mercury, one carbon, to Cibatio (feeding), and the jokey thermometer line was dropped.

**Rules fixed:**
- Soot had no jar, so the cut piece now leaves as carbon dioxide.
- Lapis was cut: it copies another game's stone card.
- Every sigil is earned by a finding made in play and stays hidden until the basics are in place.
- Every sigil fills as the molecule changes and is drawn under it in thick lines.

**The other names:** Aqua Regia, Vitriol, Sulphur, Mercurius, Chrysopoeia, Ouroboros, Coniunctio, Congelatio, Cibatio, Pelican, Palingenesis, Caduceus and Tria Prima are each the real term, used for what it means.
