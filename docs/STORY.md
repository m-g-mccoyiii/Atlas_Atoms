# Overreact: the story of the whole game

2026-11-12, brought in line with the sign on 2026-11-13 (rulings AP and AR: the yellow sign is the game's prestige; it is taken three times; each time the collection starts over and every yield is a fifth higher for good; each time the sign shows itself more). A proposal. The owner: "It would fit in with having a meta narrative for the game too" (on the King in Yellow thread of ruling AM3). Nothing here is built. The notes that carry it are in `apps/steam/js/react/notes_data.js`; how they are written is in `NOTES_WRITING.md`.

## In one breath

A dry, able chemist rents a room and makes compounds to spec. A thin book he did not order turns up on the bench. It is a play. He reads it. After that the chemistry is no longer the point: every overreaction is a look through a keyhole, and everything he collects is a step toward seeing HIS FACE. Near the end of the collection he finds the sign under the molecule, and he may take it. It is not paid in gold. It takes everything on the shelves, and he starts again with a little more yield and one stroke of yellow on the page. He does this three times. After the third the sign is whole and he sees. Then he goes on making compounds, very calmly.

## How it is told

No cutscenes. No pages of text. The player is never stopped to be told a story. It comes only through:

- the margin notes (the main carrier);
- the names and one-line entries of findings and catalog items;
- what the homunculi do in their jars;
- small changes to the title screen, the page and the music as the career goes on;
- two rare moments, a few seconds each.

The clock is the share of all unlocks owned (166 things in `tools_chem/FINDINGS.md`) and the number of signs taken (0 to 3). A run still goes from calm to wild by itself (ruling AM), but how far it can go depends on the career: a new player's run ends at "small wrong things"; a player with nearly everything starts a run already past the book. Taking the sign empties the shelves, so the share falls back to nothing, but the story does not start over: see "The three laps".

## Who he is

A jobbing chemist. Good at it, a little vain about his labelling. One rented room over a landlord who complains about the smell. A cat that is not allowed on the bench. A supplier who overcharges. He wants to meet the spec, pay the rent and keep his eyebrows. He has no name; the notebook is his and nobody else reads it.

## The thin book

- It arrives when a quarter of everything is unlocked, in a parcel from the supplier that nobody ordered. There is no invoice. The parcel is addressed in the chemist's own hand.
- It is thin and bound in a colour he will only call "buff".
- It is a play in two acts. No author is given in the game.
- Act one is harmless and rather dull. He reads it and sleeps badly.
- He puts the book on a high shelf. It keeps being on the bench.
- He reads act two when half of everything is unlocked. The game never says what is in it.
- It is not the grimoire. The grimoire is an instrument he can buy.

## What HE wants

The game never says, and the chemist guesses differently at each stage:

- at first, nothing: HE is a name in a list of characters, with no lines;
- then, to be seen: every overreaction shows a little more (a sleeve, a chin);
- then, to be paid: and the price turns out not to be gold. The sign takes the collection itself, every bottle and jar, and gives back a fifth more yield and one stroke of itself;
- at the end, it seems HE only wanted the collection made and given up three times. The catalog is the one thing the sign never takes. The collection was the second act all along, and the chemist wrote it, three times over.

The joke under all of it: the chemist treats HIM as he treats everything, as a spec to be met. "IF THE YIELD IS HIGH ENOUGH HE MUST COME. That is science."

## The three laps

The sign is taken three times (`meta.sign` 0 to 3; `P.signState` gives the page the level, the lift and what is kept). Each lap is the same climb with nothing on the shelves, yields a fifth higher and the number to beat a little higher, so a start with nothing is about as hard each time (`RIG.md`, thirteenth, part 2). What changes is how much of the sign is on the page and how the notes begin.

| Signs taken | Yield | The page | Where the notes start, and how |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | as dealt | No yellow anywhere. The sign is only the unfinished mark at the bottom of the title page. | "The job". The story as the stages above have it, up to "The sign". |
| 1 | 1.2 times | The first stroke, pale, under the molecule on every OVERREACT, gone when the page turns. One yellow tick on the yield track. One coffee stain in three dries in the shape of the stroke. | "Small wrong things", not "The job": he is sane and dry again but knows what the buff book is when it arrives, and it arrives sooner (at a tenth, not a quarter). One line now and then is in a second hand. |
| 2 | 1.4 times | Two strokes, and they stay on the page all round, very pale, under the molecule. The homunculi face them when a chain runs long. The reagent cards carry the mark small in a corner. | "The book": the book is on the bench from the first run. He stops pretending not to see the strokes. The second hand writes about one note in four. |
| 3 | 1.6 times | The sign whole, under every molecule from the first page, and on the title page in place of the unfinished mark. The lamp light on the bench is a shade warmer. | Moment two, then the "after" notes for good. |

- The stages inside a lap still open by the share of things unlocked in that lap (the story is earned by play), but each lap's floor is one stage higher and its stages come at smaller shares, so the slide is quicker each time.
- What is never taken, and so is what the story hangs on between laps: the catalog, what the first game taught, the count of signs, and the lifetime counts (set aside, shown on the bench as "before": "Rings closed: 212. Before: 1,460.").
- Never the three-armed sign of 1989: our own mark, built from our four yield marks, a stroke a lap and whole at the third.

## The two moments

1. **The second act** (once, the first page after half of everything is unlocked). The margins are empty. No doodles. The music drops to its lowest layer. One note, large, in a neat hand: "I read the second act." Play goes on as normal. On the next page the notes come back, and one of them is "It was not dull."
2. **The face** (once, the first OVERREACT after the third sign is taken). OVERREACT fills the screen as always. Then two seconds of nothing: no shake, no notes, the creatures still. One note, large and neat: "Oh. There you are." Nothing is shown. HIS FACE is never drawn, at any point in the game. Then the round ends as normal and the yield is counted.

Both are skipped by any key and never stop the player from playing.

## How it ends: three to choose from

The game must stay playable for ever after any of them. The sign empties the shelves three times; after the third nothing is taken again and nothing is ever locked. The three endings below are kept as options. Each now has to answer two things the first draft did not: why the shelves are empty after a sign, and what the third sign is.

**A. The second hand.** One chemist all the way. Taking the sign is his own act: he gives the collection up ("Cleared the shelves myself. Could not say why. Could. Will not."). From the first sign a second hand writes under his now and then: short, exact comments on the player's play ("Page two was lazy." / "Better."). It writes more each lap. After the third sign and moment two his own notes are calm and the second hand is always there: HE turns out to be a close reader with opinions about yields. The bench gains a shelf where the margins of each earlier lap can be read again.

**B. The next tenant.** Each sign ends a tenancy. The chemist who takes it is gone in the morning; the room is aired, the shelves are bare, and the next run is written by a new chemist, sane and dry, who finds a notebook, a catalog, a cat and a yellow stroke on the bench that will not scrub off ("The last tenant left a catalog, ten empty jars and a cat. Decent of him. Also a stain."). The old tenants' notes show through faintly, in pencil. Each tenant slides faster than the last because each finds one more stroke waiting. The third sign is taken by the fourth tenant in all but name: after moment two the title screen shows the room with all the earlier notebooks on the shelf, and the one who goes on playing is calm.

**C. The refusal.** After the third sign there is one more choice at the start of every run: work under the sign, or turn the page face down. Face down, that run is written by the sane chemist of lap one, with HIM knocking politely in the margins and being ignored ("Someone at the door. I am titrating."), and the yield lift still applies. Under the sign the run is the calm "after" one. The player picks the voice each run.

**Which fits three laps best: B.** It is the only one that gives the reset a reason the player can see at once (a new tenant has nothing on the shelves; what is kept is what a tenant would leave behind: the catalog, the old notebooks, the mark), it makes each lap a fresh voice, so the hundred and more notes can be written three times in three tempers without the one chemist seeming to forget, and "manifests more" is literal: each tenant inherits one more stroke. Its cost is the most writing (three first acts). A is the cheapest and the funniest ending (the terrible thing at the end of the road is a critic) and its second hand can be kept inside B as the earlier tenants writing in the margins; alone, A has to explain why a sane man empties his own shelves three times. C adds a choice only after everything is over and splits the writing for good.

## Rules for everyone who adds to this

- Sources are Chambers's 1895 book and Bierce only. Our own words. Never the three-armed sign of 1989, nor anything from later writers or games. The full rule is in `NOTES_WRITING.md`.
- He is "he" (doubted) from a quarter, HE and HIM from a half, "the King" from four fifths, "the King in Yellow" only at the end.
- HIS FACE is never drawn or described.
- Funny before frightening. No gore. Nothing that stops play.
- The story must be earned by play (ruling: progression by play): a stage opens because of what the player has found and the signs taken, never because of time passed.
- The sign is never paid in gold and is never forced: it is offered when 9 tenths is held and the player may go on to everything first.
