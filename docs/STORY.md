# Overreact: the story of the whole game

2026-11-12; brought in line with the sign (rulings AP and AR) and then with the peppering and the tone (rulings AM4 and AM5) on 2026-11-13. A proposal. The owner: "It would fit in with having a meta narrative for the game too" (on the King in Yellow thread of ruling AM3). The notes that carry it are written (`apps/steam/js/react/notes_data.js`, 556 of them); how they are written is in `NOTES_WRITING.md`. Nothing else here is built.

## In one breath

A working chemist rents a room and makes compounds to spec. From his first runs, now and then, he writes down something that is slightly wrong, as plainly as he writes down a temperature, and goes on with the work. A thin book he did not order comes in a parcel. It is a play. He reads it. After that someone is in the room in the evenings, and each overreact shows a little more in the flask. He wants to see his face. Near the end of the collection he finds the sign under the molecule and may take it. It takes everything on the shelves and gives a fifth more yield and one stroke of yellow on the page. He does this three times. After the third the sign is whole and he sees. Then he goes on making compounds.

## How it is told

No cutscenes. No pages of text. The player is never stopped to be told a story. It comes only through:

- the margin notes (the main carrier);
- the names and one-line entries of findings and catalog items;
- what the homunculi do in their jars;
- small changes to the title screen, the page and the music as the career goes on;
- two rare moments, a few seconds each.

**It is peppered, not gated** (ruling AM4). The thread does not wait for a share of unlocks or for a lap of the sign. A new player meets a first faint oddity in the first two or three runs: one line in a run, easy to miss, never explained. After that a thread line comes at a slow, uneven rate that rises over the career: about one note in seven or eight in the first hour, one in five or six by five hours, one in four by twenty, one in three and a half on the laps (`NOTES_WRITING.md`, "How often"). Most notes are always about the work.

**It comes from six sources**, so no one clock holds it back: late rounds of any run; things that happen (an overreact, a long chain, a lost round, a purchase, sulfur on the molecule); the creatures owned, who notice before he does; lifetime counts (the hundredth reagent, the tenth lost run); the share of unlocks and the runs played; and the laps of the sign.

**The tone** (rulings AM4 and AM5) is a real notebook. Nothing is loud and nothing is a quip. A wrong thing is recorded with a number or a direction in it, he does not comment on it, and the next note is about glassware.

## Who he is

A jobbing chemist. Good at it, careful about his labels. One rented room over Mr Pike, the landlord, who complains about the smell. A cat that gets on the bench. A supplier, Dunmore, who overcharges. A mother he owes a letter. He wants to meet the spec and pay the rent. He has no name; the notebook is his and nobody else reads it, until the third lap.

## The thin book

- It comes once he is on the second rung of the ladder (an eighth of everything unlocked, or eight runs played), in Dunmore's parcel with the glassware. It is not on the invoice. The parcel was posted from his own street.
- It is thin and bound in a buff yellow cloth. The dye comes off on the fingers.
- It is a play in two acts. No author is given in the game.
- He reads act one over supper and is up at four.
- He puts the book on the top shelf behind the acid bottles. It is on the bench again in the morning.
- He reads act two on the third rung (35 in 100 unlocked, or twenty-five runs). The game never says what is in it.
- It is not the grimoire. The grimoire is an instrument he can buy.
- The sign does not take it: when the shelves are cleared, the book stays on the bench.

## What he wants, and what the King wants

The chemist wants to see his face. The game never says what the King wants, and the chemist tries one thing after another, as a chemist would:

- at first, nothing: there is a name in the cast list with no lines in act one;
- then he tries yield: longer chains show more (a sleeve at nine reflexes, a shoulder at eight, a chin at the top mark);
- then he tries gold: he leaves the tin on the sill overnight with a card saying what he wants. It is all there in the morning. He rewrites the card to ask for less;
- then the sign: it is not paid in gold. It takes the collection itself, every bottle and jar;
- at the end it seems the King wanted the collection made and given up three times, and the catalog read aloud. The catalog is the one thing the sign never takes.

The chemist treats him as he treats everything, as a spec to be met: "Higher yields bring the King nearer; moved the chalk mark again."

## The three laps

The sign is taken three times (`meta.sign` 0 to 3; `P.signState` gives the page the level, the lift and what is kept). Each lap is the same climb with nothing on the shelves, yields a fifth higher and the number to beat a little higher (`RIG.md`, thirteenth). The share of unlocks falls to nothing, but the voice does not go back: each lap changes it.

One yellow tick on the yield track. | **He knows.** The shelf is bare and he has chalked where each thing goes. The oddities come back and he records them without surprise. - A thread line is about one note in three and a half on the laps, a little more in the late rounds, and never two together.
- The creatures hatched again on a lap have not forgotten: "Hydrogen hatched and went to the old mark on the ceiling."
- What is never taken, and so is what the story hangs on between laps: the catalog, the thin book, what the first game taught, the count of signs, and the lifetime counts (set aside, shown on the bench as "before").
- Never the three-armed sign of 1989: our own mark, built from our four yield marks, a stroke a lap and whole at the third.

## The two moments

1. **The second act** (once, the first page after the third rung is reached). The margins are empty. No doodles. The music drops to its lowest layer. One note in a neat hand, the ordinary size: "Read the second act last night, between ten and four." Play goes on as normal. On the next page the notes come back, and one of them is "Bench work from seven as usual, no sleep."
2. **The face** (once, the first OVERREACT after the third sign is taken). OVERREACT fills the screen as always. Then two seconds of nothing: no shake, no notes, the creatures still. One note, large and neat: "Saw his face at the top mark tonight." Nothing is shown. His face is never drawn or described, at any point in the game. Then the round ends as normal and the yield is counted.

Both are skipped by any key and never stop the player from playing.

## How it ends: three to choose from

The game must stay playable for ever after any of them. The sign empties the shelves three times; after the third nothing is taken again and nothing is ever locked.

**A. The second hand.** One chemist all the way. Taking the sign is his own act. On the third lap a second hand writes under his: short, exact corrections of the player's play. After moment two his own notes are calm and the second hand is always there: the King turns out to be a close reader with opinions about yields. **The notes as written are this ending.**

**B. The next tenant.** Each sign ends a tenancy. The chemist who takes it is gone in the morning and the next run is written by a new chemist who finds a notebook, a catalog, a cat and a yellow stroke on the bench. It gives the reset a reason the player sees at once. It would need the notes written three times in three voices, and the lap lines that say "as in the first notebook" changed.

**C. The refusal.** After the third sign there is one more choice at the start of every run: work under the sign, or turn the page face down. Face down, that run is written by the chemist of the first time through, and the yield lift still applies.

Another writer's note on this page judged B the best fit for three laps. I wrote A because the brief for the laps was one chemist whose voice changes (he knows; he is polite; another hand). The owner has not chosen.

## Rules for everyone who adds to this

- Sources are Chambers's 1895 book and Bierce only. Our own words. Never the three-armed sign of 1989, nor anything from later writers or games. The full rule is in `NOTES_WRITING.md`.
- The ladder: an oddity from the first run; the book and "someone" on the second rung; "he" and his face on the third; "the King" on the fourth; "The King in Yellow" only on the fifth.
- His face is never drawn or described. No capitals for him.
- Tone (AM4, AM5): never loud, never trying to be scary, never a quip. Plain, specific, unperformed. No gore. Nothing that stops play.
- The story is earned by play (ruling: progression by play): a rung opens because of what the player has found, the runs played and the signs taken, never because of time passed.
- The sign is never paid in gold and is never forced: it is offered when 9 tenths is held and the player may go on to everything first.
