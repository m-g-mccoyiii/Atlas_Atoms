# Overreact: the story of the whole game

2026-11-12. A proposal. The owner: "It would fit in with having a meta narrative for the game too" (on the King in Yellow thread of ruling AM3). Nothing here is built. The notes that carry it are in `apps/steam/js/react/notes_data.js`; how they are written is in `NOTES_WRITING.md`.

## In one breath

A dry, able chemist rents a room and makes compounds to spec. A thin book he did not order turns up on the bench. It is a play. He reads it. After that the chemistry is no longer the point: every overreaction is a look through a keyhole, and everything he collects is a step toward seeing HIS FACE. When everything is found, he sees. Then he goes on making compounds, very calmly.

## How it is told

No cutscenes. No pages of text. The player is never stopped to be told a story. It comes only through:

- the margin notes (the main carrier);
- the names and one-line entries of findings and catalog items;
- what the homunculi do in their jars;
- small changes to the title screen, the page and the music as the career goes on;
- two rare moments, a few seconds each.

The clock is the share of all unlocks owned (166 things in `tools_chem/FINDINGS.md`). A run still goes from calm to wild by itself (ruling AM), but how far it can go depends on the career: a new player's run ends at "small wrong things"; a player with everything starts a run already past the book.

## Who he is

A jobbing chemist. Good at it, a little vain about his labelling. One rented room over a landlord who complains about the smell. A cat that is not allowed on the bench. A supplier who overcharges. He wants to meet the spec, pay the rent and keep his eyebrows. He has no name; the notebook is his and nobody else reads it.

## The thin book

- It arrives when a quarter of everything is unlocked, in a parcel from the supplier that nobody ordered. There is no invoice. The parcel is addressed in the chemist's own hand.
- It is thin and bound in a colour he will only call "buff".
- It is a play in two acts. No author is given in the game.
- Act one is harmless and rather dull. He reads it and sleeps badly.
- He puts the book on a high shelf. It keeps being on the bench.
- He reads act two when half of everything is unlocked. The game never says what is in it.
- It is not the grimoire. The grimoire is an instrument he can buy.

## What HE wants

The game never says, and the chemist guesses differently at each stage:

- at first, nothing: HE is a name in a list of characters, with no lines;
- then, to be seen: every overreaction shows a little more (a sleeve, a chin);
- then, to be paid: gold is yellow, heavy and patient, and the last sigil takes it;
- at the end, it seems HE only wanted a full catalog read aloud. The collection was the second act all along, and the chemist wrote it.

The joke under all of it: the chemist treats HIM as he treats everything, as a spec to be met. "IF THE YIELD IS HIGH ENOUGH HE MUST COME. That is science."

One-line entries can carry it too. A few, to show the tone (not built):

- Finding, at about 30%: "Did not open the book for a whole run."
- Finding, at about 50%: "Read to the end of something."
- Catalog entry for adamantane, the last one: "A cage of four six-rings. Whatever was in it has left."

Lifetime counts tell it as well, in the same dry voice on the bench: "Overreactions: 212. Looks through the keyhole: 212." from the halfway mark; before that only the first number is shown.

## The two moments

1. **The second act** (once, the first page after half of everything is unlocked). The margins are empty. No doodles. The music drops to its lowest layer. One note, large, in a neat hand: "I read the second act." Play goes on as normal. On the next page the notes come back, and one of them is "It was not dull."
2. **The face** (once, the first OVERREACT after everything is unlocked). OVERREACT fills the screen as always. Then two seconds of nothing: no shake, no notes, the creatures still. One note, large and neat: "Oh. There you are." Nothing is shown. HIS FACE is never drawn, at any point in the game. Then the round ends as normal and the yield is counted.

Both are skipped by any key and never stop the player from playing.

## How it ends: three to choose from

The game must stay playable for ever after any of them. Nothing is locked or reset.

**A. The second hand.** After moment two the chemist's notes are calm, and now and then a second hand writes under his: short, exact comments on the player's play ("Page two was lazy." / "Better."). HE turns out to be a close reader with opinions about yields. The player can still do everything; the second hand gives every later run a dry audience, and the bench gains a shelf where the margins of each earlier stage can be read again.

**B. The next tenant.** After moment two the title screen shows the room empty and aired, the notebook shut on the bench. The next run is written by a new chemist, sane and dry, with all unlocks kept ("The last tenant left his glassware, ten jars and a cat. Decent of him."). The old chemist's notes show through faintly now and then, in pencil. The slide begins again, faster, with new lines.

**C. The refusal.** The last sigil gives one more choice at the start of every run: pay the sign, or burn the book for gold. Burn it and that run is written by the sane chemist, with HIM knocking politely in the margins and being ignored ("Someone at the door. I am titrating."). Pay it and the run is the calm "after" one. The player picks the voice each run.

**I would pick A.** It keeps one chemist and one voice, it costs the least to build (one more list of notes), it gives the player something new on every later run, and it is the funniest: the terrible thing at the end of the road is a critic. B is the best if the owner wants the notes to start afresh for a second career. C splits the writing in two for good.

## Rules for everyone who adds to this

- Sources are Chambers's 1895 book and Bierce only. Our own words. Never the three-armed sign of 1989, nor anything from later writers or games. The full rule is in `NOTES_WRITING.md`.
- He is "he" (doubted) from a quarter, HE and HIM from a half, "the King" from four fifths, "the King in Yellow" only at the end.
- HIS FACE is never drawn or described.
- Funny before frightening. No gore. Nothing that stops play.
- The story must be earned by play (ruling: progression by play): a stage opens because of what the player has found, never because of time passed.
