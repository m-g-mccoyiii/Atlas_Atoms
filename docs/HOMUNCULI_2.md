# Overreact: the homunculi, second try

2026-11-11. The owner rejected the first set (`HOMUNCULI.md`) as reactions with a creature attached. Three designers each designed ten from a different angle and a judge picked the best of each. Nothing here is built, measured or agreed by the owner. They are being taken to the owner one at a time.

## Water, as taken to the owner

The judge found the plain flood weak: the second zip repeats steps, and a repeat pays a quarter. Changed so that it is fed and so that its steps count on:

- It is fed by water coming off the molecule in the round (dehydrate, the burner). Three waters and it is ready.
- When ready, on the page the player chooses, after that page fires: every C=C not on a benzene ring becomes C-OH, every ring a zip closed opens, and the zip runs again.
- The steps of that second zip count on from the first. A page of five steps flooded is steps six to ten.
- To be measured: how often it is ready, and how far it moves a round's total.

## The judge's picks

**Picks (one per substance)**

**1. Water, Design A.**
- Pick a page after it fires. Every C=C not on a benzene ring becomes C-OH, every closed zip ring opens, and the zip runs again.
- It deepens a run because C=C has to be kept in stock for the one flood page.
- It breaks if the new OH sit out of reach of any unspent C=O. The rerun then repeats steps and pays a quarter. It also eats the C=C that snap needs. Drop C=N, because no molecule has it. Design C's three-waters feeding is a good limiter, but it adds a second system.

**2. Hydrogen, Design B.**
- After a page fires, one circle of that page gets one use back.
- It is the only one of the ten that changes uses instead of the molecule. A player can plan a fourth firing of the best reagent, so a run is built around one reagent.
- It breaks if the next deal does not hold that reagent. Design A's reset and Design C's wipe of C=O are both worse. The balloon on a string tied to a circle suits the rule.

**3. Carbon dioxide, Design B, with a plain rule.**
- Pick a bare CH3 end. It becomes CH2-C(=O)OH for the round.
- That gives a chosen far C=O for a chain to run to, and it costs one carbon of size.
- It breaks if no bare end exists, and the extra carbon can push a gap from 3 to 4. Drop the special "counts as C=O" wording. The acid already carries a C=O, so the zip needs no new code beyond that group. Design A hits every end, which is less of a choice.

**4. Acid fumes, Design A.**
- Until the page turns, a zip may close a ring of seven or eight atoms.
- It shows what a gap is worth, so the player plans gaps instead of fixing them.
- It breaks if it is too strong, because it removes most gaps on that page. Rings of seven or eight are not in the rules yet and need measuring. Design B only works when weld or snap is in the pool, which is rare.

**5. Ammonia, Design A.**
- Every pair of C=O with 4 or 5 carbons between them welds. The second C=O of each pair becomes OH on the new ring, and the zip runs from it.
- The player holds C=O back and then spends them all at once, which is a visible big act.
- It breaks because it is the weld reagent applied everywhere, and weld was measured as not lengthening cascades. It also uses two C=O per weld. I picked A because Design B's C=N is not in the code and Design C's flat ring cannot zip.

**6. Wood spirit, Design B.**
- Pick one OH. It is capped for the round and every reagent skips it. A zip still starts from it.
- The player can oxidize wildly around one protected start, so the chain survives a loose line.
- It breaks if it is the wrong OH, and each cap adds a carbon. Design B's wording is unclear on this point. A capped OH should still start a zip, and the rule must say so.

**7. Salt, Design B, with a limit.**
- Pick a page. The rings it closed stay closed all round.
- Later pages count their steps on from the kept steps, but by at most 2.
- The player chooses which page is the banked page and which is the engine.
- It breaks because step n pays 50 times n. With no limit, a three-step page followed by a three-step page pays steps 4 to 6, which is far too much. Kept rings also use up their C=O for the round.

**8. Brimstone, Design B, with Design C's free choice.**
- Pick two carbons 3 to 6 apart that are not in a ring. One S joins them, and the S counts as one carbon on a zip path.
- Far ends become neighbors, so the player builds the shape as a loop, and the real rubber-bridge chemistry fits.
- It is the strongest pick. It breaks if the new ring blocks a later zip ring, and it costs size. S is new to the code and must not count as a zip ring.

**9. Ethylene, Design A.**
- After a page fires, every bare CH3 end becomes CH=CH2.
- It plants C=C for hydrate, burette and snap.
- It barely passes. It is one dealt reaction aimed at more places, and the grow reagent is close to it.

**10. Carbon monoxide, Design B.**
- Pick a chain bond not in a ring. C-C becomes C-C(=O)-C.
- A gap of seven carbons splits into 3 and 4, both within reach. This is the real insertion of CO.
- It breaks by growing the molecule by one carbon, and it can place a C=O anywhere. Cap it at once a round. It beats Design A's hydroformylation, which only works on terminal C=C.

**Extra:** peroxide (Design B's dihydroxylation) does not pass. It makes two OH from one C=C, which hydrate and oxidize already approximate, and bleach is a weak match.

**Three weakest picks**

1. **Ethylene.** It is a bigger version of an existing reagent with one fixed outcome and no choice. It does not change how a run plays. Replace it, or add a pick of which page it ripens.
2. **Ammonia.** It duplicates weld, which was measured as not lengthening cascades, and the player has no choice of spot. It feels like a reaction pulled from the pool. A better version would be Design B's protection of a C=O from grow, but it needs C=N added to the code.
3. **Water.** The rerun pays only a quarter unless it runs longer, and plain C=C to OH makes no C=O. The best play is to leave it unused until a molecule happens to fit, which is a reaction pulled and not a plan.

**Weak spots in the strong picks:** salt and brimstone can warp scoring, and wood spirit needs one rule line about capped OH starting a zip.

## Design A: one act a round, the choice is when

**Frame.** Each is spent once a round, on the page the player picks, before the go arrow, on the whole molecule. A step pays full only if it passes the longest cascade of an earlier page. Repeats pay a quarter. The unseen part is the next page's deal.

**1. Water (fast).** A jug that tips itself.
- Every C=C not in a benzene ring becomes C-OH. Every closed zip ring opens. The zip runs again.
- Early: puts a start on every chain, but spends the C=C that dehydrate and burette need. Late: reruns the chain just built, but pays a quarter unless it runs longer.
- Run: keep your C=C until the page shows what it needs.

**2. Hydrogen (fast).** A small bobbing balloon.
- Every C=C becomes C-C. Every C=O at a chain end becomes OH.
- Early: an OH start at every end. Late: ends a page on starts for nothing, so it rarely fits there.
- Run: a safe, cheap reset for a molecule that is all double bonds.

**3. Carbon dioxide (medium).** A frost-white bird.
- Every CH3 chain end becomes CH2-C(=O)OH.
- Early: every end gains a C=O to reach, one carbon farther. Late: only if the size is under 50, since each end adds a carbon.
- Run: you spend size to buy reach.

**4. Acid fumes (medium).** A hissing green snake.
- Until the page turns, a zip may close a ring of seven or eight atoms, with the OH five or six carbons from the C=O. The zip runs.
- Early: crosses gaps before a chunk is dealt. Late: crosses the one gap left after the page's reagents. Risk: it fires only where a gap stays.
- Run: you plan gaps instead of fixing them.

**5. Ammonia (medium).** A sharp-nosed hare.
- Every pair of C=O with 4 or 5 carbons between them welds at once. The second C=O of each pair becomes OH on the new ring. The zip runs from those OH.
- Early: welds are permanent, so it sets the shape for three pages. Late: one big final weld. Risk: each weld uses two C=O.
- Run: you hold C=O back, then spend them all at once.

**6. Wood spirit (medium).** A little brown beetle.
- Every OH except the zip's start becomes O-CH3 until the round ends. Oxidize and dehydrate skip them.
- Early: two pages of free firing. Late: one page, to let a heavy oxidize line pass. Risk: each cap adds a carbon.
- Run: you can fire loose lines without losing your chain.

**7. Salt (slow).** A white crab.
- Rings closed this page stay closed at the turn. Later steps count on from the steps already kept.
- Early: a long zip builds across pages, and page two starts at step five. Late: useless, so it is only for pages one and two. Risk: kept rings cannot start again.
- Run: one huge chain, built across the round.

**8. Brimstone (slow).** A yellow toad.
- Every two CH3 chain ends, nearest first, join through one S atom. A zip that reaches one end crosses to the other.
- Early: the shape becomes a loop for all three pages. Late: a final jump across a loop. Risk: loops shrink the room left under the size limit.
- Run: the shape is built as a ring.

**9. Ethylene (medium).** A thin green stalk that nods.
- Every CH3 chain end becomes CH=CH2.
- Early: C=C everywhere for hydrate, burette and snap. Late: the same for the next round, which is useless, so it is an early or middle act.
- Run: a shape grown for later reagents.

**10. Carbon monoxide (slow).** A grey moth.
- Every terminal C=C becomes CH2-CH2-CHO, one carbon longer.
- Early: every C=C is already a C=O. Late: reaches a C=O 5 carbons off. Risk: it grows the molecule.
- Run: C=C is now a C=O in waiting.

**Might not work under CASCADES.md**
- Water's C=N to C=O is dropped because no molecule carries C=N.
- Acid fumes needs rings of seven or eight atoms on the page.
- Salt needs the step count to continue across pages. That is a new rule, and it makes steps pay more than the table says.
- The acid end made by carbon dioxide is a C=O only if zips accept it.
- Sulfur needs S in the code.
- Ammonia's weld was measured as not lengthening cascades.
- Hydrogen and ethylene are weak alone, which suits fast and medium jars.

## Design B: a standing rule

All ten are pointed right after a page is fired and before the next deal is seen. Each is once a round. Nothing here is built or measured.

1. **Water** (fast). A soaked little figure that leaves puddles.
   Rule: it floods the page just fired. Every C=C becomes C-OH, every C=N becomes C=O, every closed zip ring opens, then the zip runs again.
   Risk: it eats the C=C that hydrate and snap need.
   Run: one page becomes the flood, so the player keeps C=C in stock for it.

2. **Hydrogen** (fast). A light figure floating on a string, tied to one circle.
   Rule: one circle of the page just fired gets its use back.
   Risk: the use comes back for a reagent the next deal may not hold.
   Run: the player can spend a reagent three times and plan one extra use.

3. **Carbon dioxide** (medium). A fizzy figure that burps bubbles.
   Rule: a COOH goes on a bare chain end the player picks. For the round, a COOH end counts as a C=O for zips.
   Risk: no bare end means nothing happens.
   Run: every round gets a chosen far end for the chain to run to.

4. **Acid fumes** (medium). A half-melted figure that eats what it touches.
   Rule: one weld ring or snap ring opens at the bond picked, back to its two C=O or its three C=C. Those parts are live again.
   Risk: that ring's OH may have been the start of a zip.
   Run: weld and snap stop being one way and become loans.

5. **Ammonia** (slow). A stinking figure, with everything near it leaning away.
   Rule: one C=O becomes C=N. A zip still treats it as a C=O. Oxidize, reduce, hydrate and the burette skip it. The water figure turns it back to C=O.
   Risk: the player's own reduce or hydrate can no longer reach it if they needed to.
   Run: a C=O the player's own oxidize cannot spend, so wild lines become safe.

6. **Wood spirit** (slow). A blind-drunk figure that stumbles about.
   Rule: one OH is capped until the round ends. Reagents, pieces and the burner skip it.
   Risk: it may be the wrong OH.
   Run: the player can oxidize and burn freely around one protected start.

7. **Salt** (slow). A crusty pillar.
   Rule: every ring the page just fired closed is crusted and stays shut for the round. The next page continues from those rings.
   Risk: those C=O are used up for the round.
   Run: the player chooses which page is the engine that reopens and which is banked, and banked steps let later steps pay full.

8. **Brimstone** (slow). A small yellow figure, crouched and smoking.
   Rule: one sulfur joins two CH2 carbons 3 to 6 apart that are not already in a ring. It stays shut all round.
   Risk: the new ring can block a later zip ring, because a zip ring may share only one bond with an existing one.
   Run: the molecule is reshaped into a loop, so far ends become neighbors.

9. **Carbon monoxide** (slow). A thin grey figure that grips tight.
   Rule: C-C becomes C-C(=O)-C at a chain bond not in a ring.
   Risk: it lengthens a chain, so a gap of three can become four.
   Run: a gap of seven carbons splits into 3 and 4, both within reach. This is the opposite of the magnet, which shortens.

10. **Hydrogen peroxide** (medium). A pale figure that bleaches whatever it stands on.
    Rule: one C=C becomes C(OH)-C(OH), two OH side by side.
    Risk: it uses up the C=C. Both OH may reach the same C=O, and then one wins.
    Run: one chosen spot gives two starts, so the player plans zips from the middle of a chain.

**What might not work with CASCADES.md**
- Water's second run repeats steps, and a repeat pays a quarter. It pays well only if the new OH reach unspent C=O. Plain C=C to OH makes no C=O.
- Salt brings back the problem the reopening rule fixed, a chain with its C=O used up. It is limited to one page a round, but it needs measuring.
- Ammonia, wood spirit and peroxide all protect or place one group. Ammonia and wood spirit are too close and one may need to go.
- Acid, ammonia, wood spirit, carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide and peroxide are still one spot each. Their only change is that the result lasts the round, so the owner may judge them the same as dealt reactions.
- COOH, C=N and S are new groups that the zip code does not have yet.
- A C=N or a COOH may reopen differently from the zip ring. The rules for that are not written.

## Design C: fed during the run

Shared rule for all ten: each creature wears pips for its hunger. The named food fills a pip. When the pips are full it acts once, on the page and at the spot the player picks, then empties. The next deal is unseen.

**1. Water.** Fast jar. A soaking little figure that leaves puddles.
- Eats: 3 waters coming off.
- Rule: every C=C not on a benzene ring becomes C-OH. Every C=N becomes C=O. Every zip ring opens, then the zip runs again.
- Use: pages 1 and 2 shed water, and page 3 floods. Risk: it destroys the C=C that snap needs, and the new OH may sit out of reach.
- Run: dehydrate becomes charging.
- Might not work: the second zip on one page may pay only a quarter as a repeat step.

**2. Hydrogen.** Fast jar. A light figure on a string, like a balloon.
- Eats: 3 hydrogens, which oxidize gives off.
- Rule: every C=C not on a benzene ring becomes C-C. Every C=O becomes OH, except one the player taps.
- Use: tap the C=O at the far end, then oxidize the OHs. Risk: it wipes the C=O the chain was built from.
- Run: oxidize feeds it and it undoes oxidize.
- Might not work: a chain of OH with one C=O zips one step, so it is a reset and not a boost.

**3. Ethylene.** Medium jar. A thin green figure that ripens things.
- Eats: 3 C=C used up.
- Rule: every bare CH3 chain end grows by two carbons ending in C=C.
- Use: fire it after a gap is found. Risk: the size limit stops it.
- Run: it feeds the C=C that hydrate and the burette work on.
- Might not work: the existing grow reagent is close to this.

**4. Carbon dioxide.** Medium jar. A fizzy figure sitting on a block of dry ice.
- Eats: 2 acid ends cut off.
- Rule: at this page turn the rings stay closed.
- Use: the next zip then starts from the last OH, with no restart. Risk: those C=O are spent for the round.
- Run: the player chooses between engine pages and ladder pages.
- Might not work: no reagent cuts an acid end yet.

**5. Acid fumes.** Medium jar. A half-melted figure that eats through whatever it touches.
- Eats: 2 weld rings made.
- Rule: every weld ring opens. Its OH becomes C=O, and the second C=O comes back.
- Use: weld, open, weld again. Risk: it opens welds the player wanted kept.
- Run: weld becomes reusable, and weld was measured as not lengthening cascades.

**6. Wood spirit (methanol).** Medium jar. A blind-drunk stumbling figure.
- Eats: 1 reagent that found nothing.
- Rule: every OH is capped for the round except the ones the player taps. Reagents and the burner skip capped OH.
- Use: protect the zip start before oxidize. Risk: a capped OH cannot be used.
- Run: wasted reagents are no longer pure loss.

**7. Carbon monoxide.** Slow jar. A silent, pale figure that gets close to everything.
- Eats: 2 chain-end C=O lost.
- Rule: every C=C not on a benzene ring becomes C-C, with a new carbon beside it that is a C=O.
- Use: after the burner or hydrate has made C=C. Risk: the size limit.
- Run: it plants C=O along the whole shape.
- Might not work: no reagent gives off carbon monoxide yet, so the feed needs a source. The picture is hydroformylation.

**8. Ammonia.** Slow jar. A little figure that stinks and makes everything lean away.
- Eats: 2 C=O left unused at a page end.
- Rule: every pair of C=O 3 or 4 carbons apart closes into a ring with N in it, and the pair is used up.
- Use: when the pair is dead weight. Risk: it takes C=O a zip needed.
- Run: a lazy C=O becomes a ring, and it forces leaving C=O unused on purpose.
- Might not work: the ring is flat and cannot zip, and it may only remove steps.

**9. Salt.** Slow jar. A crusty pillar that grows by repeating itself.
- Eats: one cascade of 4 steps or more.
- Rule: the player picks two bonds on the chain. The stretch between them is copied once, inserted after itself in the same direction.
- Use: copy a stretch holding OH and C=O. Risk: the size limit, and the copy can sit in the wrong order.
- Run: a long zip is stretched into a longer one.
- Might not work: it works like the mirror, but copies and does not reflect.

**10. Brimstone (sulfur).** Slow jar. A yellow figure with a sulfur-smelling cloud, tying things together.
- Eats: 2 stretches of 6 or more plain carbons left uncrossed.
- Rule: the player picks two carbons. They are joined through one S, closing a ring.
- Use: tie a distant C=O to the OH of a chain. Risk: the ring may shorten the wrong chain.
- Run: it is a bridge for any shape.
- Might not work: the S atom is new to the code, and a S ring must not count as a zip ring.

### Hydrogen: shown to the owner, not yet answered

- Fed by hydrogen coming off, which happens when an OH becomes C=O (oxidize). Three and it is ready.
- When ready, on the page the player chooses: where the zip stops at a stretch too long to cross, the first C=O on the far side becomes OH, and the zip goes on from there. The rings count on.
- It costs that one C=O. It acts in the middle of a cascade, which no dealt reaction can.
- Why it deepens a run: a long stretch is the main thing that stops a cascade. With hydrogen the player may leave one and spend reactions on length. Feeding it needs oxidize, which kills the chain if fired before the zip.
- Not the judge's pick (a use given back to one circle): the owner turned down ones that do not touch the molecule.
- To be measured.
