# Overreact: the harness

Every design, every build and every message to the owner is checked against this page before it goes out. It is the owner's rulings, gathered in one place. Where a ruling is quoted it is in the owner's words. If something fails a line here, it is fixed before anyone sees it.

## What the game is

1. A turn: the player lines up three reactions and fires. Then whatever the molecule can do by itself, it does, and what that leaves may set off more. Nobody presses anything during it.
2. "The whole point is to have every run be different and then use all of the player elements to change the odds or interactions between 3 reactions."
3. Six reactions at the start is right. The pool grows to fifteen to twenty over many runs. One run carries only a few (first number: eight), like Vampire Survivors.
4. Closing a ring is ONE thing a molecule can do by itself. It is not the step of the game. Self-reactions are a set of pieces, unlocked by play, that chain back and forth.
5. The game is built like a pyramid: every element (reactions, self-reactions, what comes off, helpers, creatures, coins, unlocks) is made from the ones under it and feeds back into them. For every piece say: what it is made from, what it feeds, how it is unlocked.
6. Long chains come "by a combination of strategy and chance". Reactions dealt each turn are random from the player's pool; unlocks, helpers, creatures and coins change the pool, the odds of the deal, or how the three interact.
7. Reactions come in sizes unlocked by play (small changes, then bigger pieces attached, then joining far ends). Heavier compounds as an upgrade path. Real compounds (cholesterol and the like) as things to make.
8. A fun, insane, fast game. Zany long plays. Not a textbook. Not like cheap mobile game ads.

## Reactions the player fires

9. Each is a real reaction with its real name and ONE exact change that happens everywhere it fits. Never say "reacts" without naming the change: which group becomes which.
10. Each must differ from the others in what it leaves for the other reactions and for the self-reactions. Two that leave the same thing in the same place are one reaction.
11. A reaction is not a helper, a creature or a self-reaction. Those must never be "just a reaction that can be pulled".

## Helpers, creatures, coins, tiers

12. Helpers are lab objects and small real molecules. A big physical act on the molecule that does what the real object does, at a place the player chooses or on the whole structure. Unlocked for good by one deed each, then offered in runs and bought with coins; better ones cost more.
13. Creatures (homunculi) hatch from jars filled by what comes off the molecule. They are perks: zany, big, may break chemistry, do what the substance is known for in plain terms. Fed in a round by what the player's own reactions make. The player taps them. Never wasted.
14. "Resource management that isn't punishing." A player with no coins is never stuck.
15. Tiers: a run is three rounds, then six, then nine. Each win opens the next and a new layer. Nothing later than the player's stage is shown, except one outline of the next thing with its count.
16. A new player is meant to be inexperienced. No preview of what a line will do, no hints, no solver. Learning by firing and watching is the game.
17. When the total passes the four marks the page letters: stable, unstable, volatile, overreact.
18. No daily challenge, timers, login streaks or paid currency.

## The page and the art

19. Show, don't tell: objects, icons and short tokens. No sentences on a game screen. Only instructions may be wordy. Menus are full game screens, never dialogs.
20. Never restyle the ball-and-stick molecule. Never faces on atoms. Nothing may cover the molecule.
21. Rejected looks: dark navy, uppercase letter-spaced mono labels, serif headings, glow, flat lab signage, cards like Balatro's, hard rectangles, yellow pills and banners, pulsing, bursts, particle balls, check marks, circles or discs on the molecule, stretched bonds, a "set it off" button, spin gimmicks.
22. No multiplication signs or words, middle dots or em dashes in text. American spelling.
23. Everything shipped is licensed for commercial use. No file is downloaded without asking.

## How to talk to the owner

24. Plain words. No jargon of the game or of design: not "zip", "cascade", "gate", "tier" without saying what it is, "deal", "layer", "foundation test". Say what happens to the molecule.
25. One thing at a time. A few short lines. A schematic picture with every mechanic suggested, sent as an image.
26. Never a line that labels or excuses a design ("this breaks chemistry, it is a perk").
27. Do not list problems. Fix them and say what was fixed. If there is an issue, evaluate it and resolve it.
28. Do not echo the owner's examples back as the design. "You are a video game designer": do the design work, and assess the owner's ideas analytically.
29. Do not ask the owner to play until it is polished and fun.
30. State plainly what is built, what is only on paper and what was measured where. Never claim what was not seen.

## How to work

31. Many sub-agents at once. Designers measure before proposing. An independent reviewer who was not told what changed judges every piece of work.
32. Only one agent writes a given file at a time, and only one uses the browser at a time.
33. Work only on branch `steam`. Never commit to main. Never touch the deployed site.
