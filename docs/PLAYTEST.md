# Playthrough, 2026-11-10

One run of three rounds and three more first rounds, played by a script that takes equipment when offered, picks the plain round, twists when it can and otherwise takes the first reaction that carries the row on. Every decision was logged.

## What the log shows

1. The round plays itself. Of 37 decisions, 30 had exactly one reaction that carried the row on, or none. The order was hydrate, oxidize, grow on every page of every round. The other option was nearly always dehydrate, which undoes the last step. There is no decision in a round yet.
2. The number to beat is close to random. Round 1 asked for 5,700, 57,000, 7,900 and 57,000 on four different molecules. The player made 40,289, 10,523, 165 and 26,486. One round was beaten seven times over and three were lost, by the same play.
3. A twist could end the round. Closing a ring on the second reaction left nothing any reagent could act on, and the round ended at 165. (Fixed: a twist is now offered only if a reagent in hand can act afterwards.)
4. A twist breaks the row in practice. After it, no reagent follows on, so twisting costs the row it was meant to feed.
5. A round is 7 to 9 decisions and most of the total arrives in the last chain: 11,405 before it and 40,289 after in one round.
6. Three of four first rounds were lost.

## What the screens show

- Take one: three lines of explanation under each offer, reagents shown as a word with no object, "best" cut off at the page edge. (Words trimmed, "best" moved.)
- Round over: the grown molecule is drawn folded into two "C6" beads, so what was built cannot be seen.
- Banner: collided with the flask and the reagents wherever it was moved. (Now small, in a place of its own at the bottom.)
- A large molecule becomes a small dense tangle; figures print over each other above it.

## What follows

The first finding is the one that matters. Effects, pace and art sit on top of a round in which the player has nothing to choose. The next work is the rules of a page: each state of the molecule needs at least two reactions that carry the row on and lead to different places, with a reason to prefer each.
