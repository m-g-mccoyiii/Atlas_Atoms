# Overreact: the whole arc

Written 2026-11-10, revised the same day after an independent review. This is the plan for the game from the first minute to the stone. Sources for claims about other games are in `ROGUELIKE_STUDY.md`.

## Three rules

1. **One new thing at a time**, and only after the last thing is covered. Nothing later than the player's stage is shown, not even greyed out.
2. **A new layer arrives as an object, not a message.** A piece drops onto the shelf. A circle is chalked on the page. Nobody explains it.
3. **Nothing slows a round.** No animation is waited on for more than a third of a second. Every word on the page is a token.

## The frame: the Great Work

Alchemy describes its work in four stages. They are the four stages of the game. The names are for us; the player never sees them. The page changes instead: a clean lab book at the start, chalk circles and something alive on the shelf by the end.

| Stage | Old name | The player is learning | What opens | Hours, estimated |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Nigredo | to react a molecule and keep a row | the bare game | 0 to 1 |
| II | Albedo | to build with equipment | the lab shelf, suppliers, by-products | 1 to 4 |
| III | Citrinitas | to bend the rules | the alchemist, the occult shelf, the seals and the circle | 4 to 12 |
| IV | Rubedo | to master it | familiars, the pact, the stone | 12 to 30 |
| V | the second book | nothing new: reasons to start again | endless, seeds, the daily, records, the collection, new molecules | 30 and on |

About thirty hours to the stone. The review was right that the first draft's hundred hours was not backed by the content. Hours beyond thirty come from the pact, the endless run and content added later.

## A run

Now a run goes on until a number is missed. It has no end and no shape. The plan:

- **Nine rounds in three acts of three.** The third round of each act is an **assay**: a round whose rule attacks the build (few uses, two pages, no repeats, one family only). Its rule is visible from the first round of the act.
- **A choice after every round:** the take-one-of-three.
- **Beating round nine wins the run.** The page then offers to go on, endless.
- **Length:** twenty to thirty minutes, the range every game in the study lands in.
- **A lost run always pays:** credit for every round beaten, and progress on every deed.

## Stage I: the bare game

On the page: a molecule, four reagents, the total, points times multiplier, the number to beat.

Learned by doing, one per round until each has happened once:

1. A reagent changes every place it fits. Places make points.
2. Acting on what was just made keeps the row. The multiplier climbs.
3. A row of three at the end of a page overreacts: the molecule goes on by itself.

The take-one-of-three offers reagents only. Not on the page: equipment, suppliers, water and hydrogen, family names, seals, familiars.

**Opens stage II:** beating the first assay (round three) once.

## Stage II: equipment and suppliers

Each arrives alone, the first time its trigger happens:

| Layer | Arrives when | Seen as |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment | stage II opens | one lab piece among the three to take |
| Families | the player first closes a ring, makes an arene or the like | the family's name, with its token |
| By-products | three water or hydrogen are first collected | the beaker or balloon, full |
| Suppliers | the first run of stage II ends | the suppliers' page, with credit to spend |
| Reagent families | the second supplier is open | three more to open |

The lab shelf has about thirty pieces. The alchemy and occult shelves do not exist for the player yet.

**Opens stage III:** beating the second assay (round six) twice. Not a win. The review's point: a first win is the hardest thing in the early game and its difficulty is not yet measured, so the game's central hook must not sit behind it.

## Stage III: the occult

A chalk circle appears on the page under the molecule, empty. The alchemist's shelf appears among the suppliers. From here the game turns from chemistry to alchemy and the art turns with it.

### The seals

The six sigils built so far (plain, glass, occult, lean, long, hoard) are run terms with shapes drawn on them. They are not occult and they do not transmute. They are replaced by seals.

Alchemy ties seven metals to seven planets. Each seal is the planet's glyph in a double ring, chalked by hand, and each grants **one transmutation: a reaction the player cannot do any other way.** A seal carries no other terms.

| Seal | Metal | Transmutation on a full circle | Token |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saturn | lead | one atom turns heavy: it scores double wherever it is touched, for the rest of the run | heavy |
| Mars | iron | two double bonds snap into a cage | cage |
| Venus | copper | a ring closes | ring |
| Mercury | quicksilver | the molecule changes places with its mirror form: every group moves to the other end, and all of it counts as freshly made | flip |
| Jupiter | tin | the molecule doubles: a copy of the whole chain joins its end | double |
| Luna | silver | the last group made is copied onto a second place | copy |
| Sol | gold | one atom turns to gold: no reaction can change it again, and it pays double whenever a neighbor is touched | gold |

- **One seal is taken into a run.** It is the circle under the molecule.
- **The circle:** a reagent lights a rune the first time it is used; a broken row puts out the last rune; a full circle performs the transmutation and starts again. This is built, with a multiplier where the transmutation will be.
- **Earning them:** in any order, each by its own deed. No ladder. The first seal is given when stage III opens; which one depends on how the player got there (most rings closed gives Venus, most cages gives Mars, otherwise Saturn).

| Seal | Deed |
|---|---|
| Saturn | given, or a round beaten with three reagents |
| Mars | a round with no broken row |
| Venus | ten rings closed |
| Mercury | a row of ten |
| Jupiter | a molecule of thirty atoms |
| Luna | the same reagent used five times in one row |
| Sol | a win |

Four seals ship first: Saturn, Mars, Venus, Mercury. Jupiter, Luna and Sol follow once those four are balanced.

### The shelves

The alchemist sells the alchemy shelf. Brenda sells the occult shelf and appears once two seals are held.

**Opens stage IV:** three seals held and one run won.

## Stage IV: mastery

### Familiars

A familiar is a creature that lives on the page beside the molecule and does one thing by itself, and is seen doing it. It does not walk across the page: it sits at the molecule and acts in under a third of a second. One is taken into a run. Each is earned by its own deed, in any order.

The owner asked for about ten. The review called six of the first ten filler, because they only moved a number. Four ship first, each with an act the player can watch change the molecule or the page:

| Familiar | What it does | Earned by |
|---|---|---|
| Homunculus | does the last reaction of each page again | given when stage IV opens |
| Salamander | a chain that overreacts goes two reactions further | a chain of twenty |
| Golem | holds one broken row a round | a win with the Mars seal |
| Moth | sits on the heaviest atom; that place counts twice | a win with the Saturn seal |

Six more follow only when each has an act as visible as these: raven, black cat, toad, rat, imp, snail are the candidates and need better rules first. The homunculus, raven, black cat and intern leave the equipment shelf when familiars arrive.

### The pact

After the first win the player may add named conditions to a run for more credit: glass (a break ends the round), lean (three reagents), hoard (no equipment offered after the start), short (two pages), blind (the number to beat is hidden). Each is an object on the start page that is turned over, with one token on it. Nothing in the game is locked behind the pact.

### The stone

Winning a run with the Sol seal makes the philosopher's stone. That is the end of the arc. The endless run and the pact stay for those who go on.

## Stage V: the second book (after the stone)

The owner asked whether four stages are enough. Four stages get a player to the stone in about thirty hours. What keeps a game played and watched after that is a fifth stage that is not a gate but a set of reasons to start another run.

| Layer | What it is | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Endless | after round nine the run goes on, the number rising every round | the uncapped score is the thing people clip and compare |
| Seeds | every run has a seed that can be typed in; the same seed gives the same molecules and the same offers | two people can play the same run and compare; a streamer's audience can play the streamer's run |
| The daily | one seed a day, the same for everyone, first attempt counted, with a local record | a reason to come back that is not a streak: missing a day costs nothing |
| The record book | best total, longest chain, biggest single reaction and furthest round, kept for each seal | a high score for every way of playing, so one record does not end the chase |
| The collection | a page of every piece, seal and familiar met so far, blank where one has not been found | Balatro's collection; a visible list of what is left |
| The grid | every seal against every pact level, a mark where a run was won | Balatro's decks and stakes; optional, and nothing is locked behind it |
| New molecules | classes that react in new ways: peptides that grow by coupling, monomers that run away into polymers | fresh content on the same rules, the thing the study found extends a game's life most |

### What makes a run worth watching

From what is on record about how Balatro and Vampire Survivors spread:

1. **Readable at a glance.** A viewer who arrives mid-run must see what is happening. This is the five-second rule already in the brief.
2. **A question on screen: will it reach the number?** One analysis of Balatro puts its tension there. Overreact has the same question every round; the assay sharpens it.
3. **Every run looks different.** Seal, familiar and five pieces of equipment out of sixty must produce runs that do not resemble each other. The balance matrix is what proves or disproves this.
4. **A moment worth clipping.** The overreact chain and a transmutation are that moment. The score must have no cap and must be allowed to get absurd.
5. **Short, and instant to restart.** A streamer drops a short cheap game into a rotation. One key starts a new run.
6. **A low price.** Vampire Survivors spread at three dollars.
7. **Something the audience can do.** A seed on screen lets a viewer play the same run.

Streamers did not find Balatro by accident: its publisher listed streamers who had played similar games and approached small ones first, and one streamer took it from 183 wishlists to about 30,000 in six weeks. That is marketing, not design, and it is outside this document.

## What never happens

No timers, no login streaks, no paid currency. No unlock sits behind top difficulty. Every deed can be advanced in a lost run. No character speaks, and no screen explains.

## Order of work

1. **Stages.** One saved number for the player's stage; every layer checks it before it shows.
2. **The nine-round run with assays.** It gives a run a shape and a win.
3. **The balance matrix**, before any more content: solo worth of all sixty pieces, all pairs, and the numbers to beat for nine rounds. A test run scored 1,189,899 against 28,000.
4. **Four seals** with their transmutations, replacing the six sigils.
5. **Four familiars.**
6. **The pact.**
7. **The last three seals and the stone.**
8. **Seeds, the daily and the record book.** Seeds come first; they need every random draw in a run to come from one seeded source.
9. **The collection and the grid.**
10. **New molecule classes.**

## What the review said that the plan does not follow

- It would cut to four or five seals for good. The plan keeps seven and ships four first, because the owner asked for depth.
- It would cut the pact, the endless run and the stone. The plan keeps them at the very end, with nothing locked behind them.
- It called the planetary metals the most obvious occult theme. The plan keeps them because they are real alchemy, each maps to a metal, and the glyphs can be drawn in chalk.
