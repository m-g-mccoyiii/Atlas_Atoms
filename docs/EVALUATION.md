# Overreact: evaluation of every aspect, 2026-11-10

Six independent reviewers each took one aspect. Each read `EVALUATOR_BRIEF.md` first. This file records what each found. The decisions drawn from them are in `BALANCE.md` and the order of work in `ARC.md`.

## 1. Readability, look and words

- **Five-second read fails on three of four screens.** Take-one: three choices of different kinds do not read as three choices; reagents are marker words and equipment is a 3D object. Choose-the-round: nothing says the red number is the target or the coins the reward. Suppliers: mostly empty, "new run" does not read as a control.
- **In a round** the total and the number to beat read. The three page ovals, the chevrons, the reaction chip and "found oxidize c=c" do not.
- **Hierarchy is wrong.** The eye hits the total, then the molecule, then the multiplier. The number to beat is the goal and is small in a corner. Equipment is nearly invisible.
- **Rejected looks found:** dark navy ink for titles and totals; yellow pills behind numbers and behind "overreact" and row names read as flat signage and as banners; the gold arc reads as a glow accent; marker words with underlines read as mobile-game word art; grey rounded chips read as web buttons.
- **The circle under the molecule** collides with the molecule and its readings. The owner asked for it there, so it stays, but it must be drawn to clear the molecule.
- **Collisions:** "beat" with the circle's prize; "+20" on the ring and chip; "found ..." over the page ovals; family name jammed against "round 1".
- **Equipment:** the twenty new pieces are bolder, larger and consistent. The forty old ones are thinner and smaller. They do not look drawn by one hand. Weakest: spatula, loupe, stopwatch, logbook, cards, transmute, pact, ouija, hood, scalepan.
- **Top changes:** reagents as 3D objects on take-one; one readout for total, multiplier and number to beat with the target large; label the page ovals; redraw the old forty to the new weight; remove yellow pills and banners; change navy to a warm dark ink.

## 2. Progression and economy

- **Credit costs are a grind after the second supplier.** At four rounds a run: trade supplier 2 runs, research 6, instrument maker 13, alchemist 26, Brenda 46. Everything costs 209 credit, about 52 runs. The best content sits at the far end.
- **Nothing to spend on after the last supplier.** The pact would pay credit into an empty shop.
- **No economy inside a run.** No currency, no reroll, no skip, no way to bin a bad piece. With five slots a bad pick is permanent.
- **Smallest fix proposed:** one run currency. A beaten round pays 1 to 3 by margin. A reroll of the three costs 1, rising each time. Skipping pays 1. Holding pays interest, 1 per 3 held, capped at 2. Binning a piece costs 2.
- **Stage gates:** round three is safe. Round six twice is the real wall and is unmeasured. Three seals and a win could slip ten hours.
- **Deeds:** several are accidents the player will never notice (the same reagent five times, thirty atoms, which seal is given first). Every deed needs a visible counter on the faint object.
- **A failed run does not feel like progress:** 1 to 3 credit against costs of 6 to 80, and nothing is left behind.
- **Replay, value for cost:** seeds and record book, endless, collection, the grid, new molecule classes.
- **Top changes:** the run currency; cap supplier costs near 40 in total and pay more on a loss; make a lost run leave something (a collection entry); counters on deeds; measure the round-six pass rate before gating on it.

## 3. Fun and watchability

- **The thrill is a climbing number, and the clip moment is the overreact chain.** It comes at most three times a round and the frames show it as a static molecule with a small tag. It does not read as spectacle.
- **Tension is dead when the number is beaten early.** One run scored 1,189,899 against 28,000; another had beaten 5,700 by page two.
- **Sixty pieces: about 32 small number bumps, 16 rule changes, 12 that could make a story.** Almost every piece triggers on "any reaction", so builds are a sum of multipliers. Only the lab coat, the cursed idol and the hand of glory link to other pieces.
- **Most exciting:** monkey's paw, the Tower, cursed idol, signed pact, ouroboros, fire extinguisher.
- **Most boring:** balance, magnet, stir plate, timer, carousel, dropper, drying tube, thermometer, fume hood, clamp, loupe, spatula.
- **Speed:** three screens before every round (take one, choose the round, and terms at run start); count-up on long chains; equipment firing one by one. Raccoin is continuous motion.
- **The hook:** every click rewrites a real molecule on the page, so a build is a chain reaction that can be seen growing. It is only partly visible in the first minute.
- **Top changes:** make the overreact chain a spectacle that speeds up as it goes and shows on the molecule; set numbers so the median player is saved on the last page; rewrite about twenty of the bumps into rules that link pieces; a visible score breakdown per piece at the end of a chain; merge the pre-round screens; a seed on screen and a share card; make the paw, the extinguisher and the Tower visible choice moments.

## 4. The core loop and decision depth

- **Real decisions per page: about 0 to 1 early, 1 to 2 late.** A fresh rack is four reagents and a page shows five, so there is no hand and no draw. A reagent either keeps the row or breaks it, so "take a continuing one" is forced. The preview of what each reagent adds then solves the rest.
- **The dominant strategy is: never break the row, take the continuing reagent with the biggest number.** A break resets the row and blocks the chain, a row survives the page turn, and most of the score arrives in the free chain. The second dominant move is to build resonance, the only family that compounds.
- **Trade-off or decoration:**
  - Equipment slots: the best trade-off in the game. Pieces that pay for breaking conflict with pieces that pay for rows.
  - Take-one-of-three: real, but several picks are strictly best (one more page, one more reaction, one more use).
  - Ring family: a real trade-off but a trap, because its bonus needs a loop and a loop breaks the row.
  - Chain, arene, sugar: small flat riders.
  - By-products: decoration. Nothing says hold, so they are spent at three.
  - The circle: weak. A row already uses different reagents, so runes light by themselves.
  - Briefs: decoration. Every brief is baked to be equally hard.
- **Against Balatro's hand:** there is no hand, three reactions are too few to plan a combination, nothing is scarce across pages, and there is no bad-luck verb. The deal retries forty times so that at least two reagents work, which removes bad luck and the puzzle with it.
- **Broken or exploitable rules found:**
  - The recycler lets loops continue and the free chain has no cap, so add-water and lose-water can ping-pong and pay every time.
  - The signed pact is a net gain of one half with no price but a slot. The hourglass is also a net gain.
  - The monkey's paw doubles the round even when the rack is too short to lose a reagent.
  - The occult term can hand out the paw before its supplier is open.
  - The clamp makes a break nearly free; with the stopper and the weights that is an exploit.
  - The numbers to beat are stale and ignore the build.
- **Top changes:**
  1. Pause the chain at forks: when two or more reagents continue it, the player picks. The biggest moment becomes a run of decisions.
  2. Make uses scarce: a set number of uses for the whole round, shared with the chain.
  3. A hand with a discard: deal four from a rack of six, one redraw a page at a cost, and drop the forty-try guarantee.
  4. A bank verb: end the row on purpose for one and a half times its worth, at the cost of the chain.
  5. Price the dominant picks and tie the number to beat to the build.
